# The 55,000-Session Problem

*Insight Timer (unsolicited redesign) — MICA graduate program · Solo: research, design, prototyping, testing · July–August 2020*

*Note: This project was part of my graduate program. The work and opinions are my own and do not reflect the views of Insight Timer.*

---

Fynn, a career-focused project manager, is finally off his last call of the day. There's one meditation session that works for him — the same one, every day; that's the point — and his streak depends on doing it tonight. He opens the app and starts navigating: past the featured content, past the new releases, past categories he's never tapped, searching for the one session he already knows he wants. The app has fifty-five thousand meditations. Right now, that's the problem. As one user put it later, in testing: "I spent a lot of time searching for a session. I do the same session every day, and I have to navigate to find it."

The app is Insight Timer, a mental-wellness platform with 10 million Listeners and 5,000 professional Guides — teachers who record and share guided meditations, bedtime stories, and prayer prompts into a library of more than 55,000 free sessions. In the summer of 2020 — COVID, wildfires, an election looming — demand for low-cost mental health tools was surging, and meditation was one of the few affordable options for people who couldn't afford therapy. My graduate program assignment was to research and redesign the app: map the workflows, build the prototypes, validate them with real users. Eight weeks, six of them for the work, and one strange luxury — no product, development, or marketing stakeholders. I never had to say no to anyone. The constraint was that the rigor had to be my own.

> **Problem:** Listeners come to Insight Timer to meditate consistently — consistency is where the mental-health benefit lives — but the library's abundance works against them. With 55,000 free sessions and no personal curation, choosing a session is tedious enough that Listeners drift to other apps, and the ones who repeat a favorite session must navigate to find it every single day. The cost falls on the people the platform exists for: users who arrive anxious and leave overwhelmed, and a business whose retention leaks precisely because its catalog grew.
>
> **Goal:** Fynn, a career-focused Listener, needs to feel accountable and motivated — able to start the right session for his mood and time of day without the search slog — validated with real users, not assumed.

I interviewed fifteen Listeners and Guides and sorted what I heard into two audience types and four personas. The Guides teach for the joy of it, not money, and post far beyond typical meditations to meet Listeners where they are. The Listeners love the free content and would pay for premium if it stayed fresh; they struggle to make meditation a habit; and the streaks and stats are what hold them accountable when motivation dips. Fynn's line summed up the tension: "We all live such busy lives it can be challenging to find time to meditate." I mapped as-is and to-be scenarios, ran lightning demos and jobs-to-be-done, and kept circling two questions: what's the perfect meditation, and what primary conditions need attention?

The answer inverted the platform's proudest number. Insight Timer's 55,000 sessions weren't the asset; they were the obstacle. Adding more content wouldn't increase retention — making the content personal would. The redesign stopped being about helping people explore the library and became about making the library disappear behind the one session that fits this person, right now.

From a Crazy 8s sprint, two ideas survived wireframing and two rounds of iteration into a connected mobile prototype. 'Today' is a homepage that skips the search-and-explore experience entirely: curated sessions based on time of day, mood, and past meditations, surfacing new Guides and skills with machine-learning recommendations so relevance doesn't calcify into a rut. 'Stats' is the accountability half — streaks, progress, and journey data that let a Listener see how far they've come. The two feed each other by design: what you do shapes Today; Today shapes what you do; Stats keeps score. I tested the prototype in five remote usability sessions over a week in August 2020 — 30 to 40 minutes each, participants ranging from under six months of meditation experience to more than five years, over pandemic internet connections that were themselves a lesson in patience.

The results were honestly mixed, and useful because of it. Every participant was satisfied with the 'Last Session' feature — the direct fix for Fynn's nightly hunt — and they loved the streak card. But 80 percent were confused by the information in Stats: "What did I unlock?" They also wanted a more obvious way to just press play. So the design moved again: a splash-screen introduction for the new data, clearer button placement, warmer greetings — 'Good Morning' instead of 'Daytime' — and richer session metadata like tradition and music-or-none. Fynn's evening now starts with a greeting and his session at the top of the screen, one tap from his streak's next day. The lesson outlived the assignment: the library never needed to be bigger. It needed to know him.
